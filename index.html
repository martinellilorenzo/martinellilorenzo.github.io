Stellar rotation, evolution & asteroseismology

I investigate the effects of rotation on stellar evolution and properties, combining stellar models with asteroseismic1, photometric and spectroscopic constraints. I use star clusters as laboratories for testing stellar models, since stars within each cluster share approximately the same age and initial chemical composition. My research aims to improve our understanding of stellar physics and the determination of stellar properties. More reliable stellar ages and compositions help reconstruct our Galaxy’s history through Galactic archaeology2; better estimates of stellar masses, radii and activity support exoplanet3 detection and characterisation. Studying stars of different ages, rotation rates and activity levels also helps test the physical processes operating in our Sun.

  1. Asteroseismology: the study of stellar oscillations to probe stars’ internal structure and rotation, and determine properties such as mass and age.
  2. Galactic archaeology: reconstructing the Milky Way’s formation and evolution from the ages, chemical compositions and motions of its stars.
  3. Exoplanets: planets orbiting stars beyond our Sun.

01Published work

Rotational mixing in star clusters

In my first-author work, we studied how different prescriptions for rotational mixing affect intermediate-mass stars. Comparing synthetic stellar populations with observations of NGC 419 and NGC 1817, we found that models with weaker rotational mixing reproduce the observed colour–magnitude diagrams more successfully.

02Ongoing PhD research

Probing internal rotation of solar-like stars with asteroseismology

My current work aims to improve asteroseismic constraints on internal rotation and the efficiency of angular momentum transport in solar-like main-sequence stars in the context of the upcoming ESA PLATO mission. I combine stellar evolution calculations with methods for extracting rotational signatures from oscillation spectra.
